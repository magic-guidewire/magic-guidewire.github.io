MAGIC

Magnetic Actuation for Guidewire Intelligent Control

  • Simulation only · no hardware calibration
  • Proximal feed 5 mm/s
  • Field amplitude 20 mT
  • V8: magnetic-head contact improved; whole-wire unloading unmet

We insert a magnetic guidewire through a complete vessel in SOFA and learn an external magnetic field from local observations, with proximal insertion held at constant speed. The aim is less magnetic-head wall contact, lower wall load, and comparable transit time. The numbers below come from checked paired runs in this repository. They are not held-out test or multi-seed generalization results.

01 — Abstract

Constant feed, local magnetic field, full vessel.

The wire is a 121-node, 400 mm elastic rod. The vessels are synthetic 3D lumens of about 270–299 mm with large and small bends, SOFA triangular wall contact and friction, and a 6 ms physics step. The main pairing keeps proximal insertion at 5 mm/s; the learned action is a 3D magnetic field only. A 7.5 mm/s constant-speed run is a speed control, not a learning gain. Field slew is limited to 0.03 T/s.

The default model was not replaced. On two development vessels, V8 reduced magnetic-head contact and magnetic-head load while transit time stayed essentially unchanged. Whole-wire load barely fell, and the realized field became more active. The pre-registered joint bar was not met.

What this page compares

Same vessel, same actuator, same 5 mm/s inlet: the learned policy versus the pre-training warm policy. Negative values mean the metric decreased. Lower contact and load are the intended direction; a higher field-rate cost is a control expense.

02 — Latest paired evaluation · V8

The magnetic head is lighter. The whole wire is not.

On 2026-10-09, 12,000 new real SOFA interactions and two same-speed full-route evaluations on development vessels finished. Both routes completed. All 18,655 physics steps met the original strict residual threshold. Whole-wire sampled penetration was 0. That is a sampled audit, not continuous non-penetration and not tissue-injury evidence. The walls remain rigid.

Why this is not a passing result

The pre-registered rule required at least a 5% drop in magnetic-head contact integral on both vessels, at least a 3% drop in whole-wire load squared integral, and limits on peak load and time. Vessel 000 head contact fell 4.665%, short of 5%. Whole-wire unloading was about 0.2% on both routes.

So qualified_improvement is false, the default model stays, and independent tests were not launched. The code flag new_learning_value only marks a directional gain on these two development pairings. It is not statistical significance.

Magnetic-head normal impulse is only about 1.76%–2.28% of whole-wire normal impulse. Local head gains are diluted by later shaft contact. Maximum proximal 5 mm/s command error is 4.7×10⁻¹² mm/s.

05 — Method and earlier rounds

Align physics and accounting before claiming learning.

01 Scene

SOFA elastic rod, complete triangular wall, friction. Magnetic gradient traction and post-solve position correction are off. The magnetic head is measured from the actual magnetic segment. Shaft wall-contact length is no longer a penalty.

02 Action

The actor sees local information and the known constant feed, and outputs a magnetic-field residual. Amplitude 20 mT, slew 0.03 T/s, residual scale 0.2. The critic may read more of the shaft and forces during training.

03 Learning

The value network is updated first. Policy updates start after enough real successful episodes. V8 used 12,000 new interactions and 3,920 updates, and actually visited 12 of 32 training-pool vessels.

04 Acceptance

Comparison starts only after both development vessels complete, every physics step converges, and force and objective terms recompute. The default model is not replaced if the pre-registered bar is missed.

Solver time

On the same ~285 mm routes, a strict residual-preserving solver cut complete simulations from about 52 minutes to 14–15 minutes. The two routes total 18,653 physics steps, all at the original residual threshold. Wall-clock ratios under different machine load are not a controlled speedup result by themselves.

Earlier pilot

A constant-field pilot reduced whole-wire contact integrals by about 2.19% / 2.77% on two vessels. Speed was roughly held. Smoothness was not consistent, and the field changed more often. That was a single training seed, not a final result.

V5 did not reduce contact

After objective-accounting repairs, same-budget learning versus warm was +1.58% / +1.36% whole-wire contact and +10.08% / +3.69% acceleration. A smoother field did not mean less wall contact. That round also did not replace the default model.

06 — What this does not show

The gain stops at the magnetic head on two development vessels.

  • Both development vessels were used repeatedly for diagnosis. This round is a single training seed. There is no independent test set and no multi-seed result, so no generalization claim.
  • Whole-wire load did not meet the pre-registered drop. The research objective is not complete.
  • The acceleration metric fell, while realized field-rate cost rose to about 3.5–3.9×. Not every smoothness measure improved.
  • 7.5 mm/s is a speed control. Transit time was shorter, but wall-load energy and peaks were higher. That is not a learned faster-and-safer policy.
  • Materials, magnetization, and coils are uncalibrated. Completing a simulated lumen is not clinical safety, and a magnetic field is not required to finish: a zero-field wire can still be passively guided in an unbranched lumen.
  • Connecting SOFA to standard reinforcement learning is not, by itself, a contribution relative to existing magnetic continuum simulation and guidewire navigation work.
